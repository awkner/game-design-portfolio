# Hiring perspective on game designer portfolios

Method note: ~13 searches/fetches. Primary hiring-side sources that were fetched in full are marked (fetched). Items from search-result summaries only are marked (snippet). Many sources are blogs, bootcamp marketing or old (2013-2018); dates are given so the writer can weigh them.

## 1. What hiring managers/leads look for, and how long they spend

### Takeaway
Reviewers want evidence of how you think (problem, decision, iteration, outcome), shown fast and playable, for work relevant to the role; the portfolio is the main deciding factor. Reliable data on time-per-portfolio does not exist: figures of 6-60 s come from unsourced career blogs; the one named-practitioner source says only "a few seconds" on the front page.

### Cited Findings
- Simon Mann (senior designer, Creative Assembly, 2018): viewers have only a few seconds, so the front page matters; avoid a demo reel on the front page, use project subsections with pictures and gameplay videos; show best work in the shortest time; avoid long design docs (40 pages mentioned); easy to spot copy-paste vs handcrafted content; non-concise CV can stop a recruiter from reading the portfolio at all (fetched) — [Game Developer, 2018](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-portfolio-building-tips-from-a-creative-assembly-vet)
- Kate Killick (game designer, Mojiworks, applicant and hiring-team member, Jan 2018): recruiters want to see how you think, so prioritise case studies; "Game screenshots on their own can't answer that." Released work is strongest, then side projects; academic and jam work less persuasive; recent work matters more; write with "I" not "we"; keep it brief; mobile-friendly site (fetched) — [PocketGamer.biz, 2018](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/11-portfolio-tips-for-game-design-graduates/)
- Ethan Levy (blogger, 2013, old): "A high quality portfolio was the single biggest factor in landing a phone interview"; his most common feedback: "You need to work on your portfolio website"; shipped games are the strongest proof; "no one is going to read a 75+ page game design document"; suggests 5-7 page treatment, feature brief with rationale, balance evaluation, UI/UX redesign, annotated balance spreadsheet, documented paper prototype, and a video of 90 s or less per piece (fetched) — [Game Developer, 2013](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/breaking-into-game-design-part-2---build-your-portfolio)
- Eileen Seemann (Lead UX Designer, Massive Entertainment, undated): show 2-4 projects you are proud of; tell a story (context, role, challenges, collaboration, learning), include unfinished ideas and alternatives; avoid big text blocks; "No one is good at everything, so pick only a handful you are really good in"; test that all links, images, videos, prototypes work (fetched; UX-design role, adjacent to game design) — [Massive](https://www.massive.se/blog/recruitment/my-top-5-portfolio-tips-ux-game-design/)
- Game Design Skills instructors (claim 1,500+ portfolios reviewed; Nathan Kellman, 1,000+ level design portfolios in 3 years): "quality beats quantity"; show systematic process (prototype, iterate, test); show collaboration (disagreements, buy-in, explaining decisions); make portfolio scannable and navigable. NOTE: sponsored article promoting their bootcamps; no independent data (fetched) — [80.lv, 13 Jan 2025](https://80.lv/articles/why-game-design-applicants-aren-t-landing-interviews)
- Elliot Pinkus (2013, old): cover letter can "convince me to move forward on a lacking resume"; don't present designs that were perfect on first pass ("you're doing it wrong"); be familiar with the studio's genre and competitors (fetched) — [Game Developer, 2013](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/tips-for-game-designer-applications-interviews)
- Time spent, unverified: ~10 s for lead reviewers (hitmarker, art-focused), ~30 s (career guides), ~60 s and ~6 s (secondhand), "3-7 minutes" (unsourced); search tool itself flagged these as low-authority (snippet) — [Hitmarker](https://hitmarker.net/career-advice/game-art-portfolio-guide), [PWSkills](https://pwskills.com/blog/school-of-design/why-portfolios-get-rejected-in-30-seconds)
- Recruiters skim first; hiring managers are usually leads with less time but expect more detailed explanation of accomplishments (snippet, generalistprogrammer.com guide, low authority) — [Generalist Programmer](https://generalistprogrammer.com/tutorials/game-developer-portfolio-and-resume-guide)
- A former BioWare hiring manager (now indie) noted hiring managers are busy and have little time for long portfolios (snippet via search; originating thread not seen) — see search result r/gameDevJobs 2020 thread: https://pol1.lr.ggtyler.dev/r/gameDevJobs/comments/fdu1lv/gamedev_portfolio_best_practices/fjkfu1b/

### Inferences
- Practical test: someone must grasp what you do, which role you target and your strongest case study within ~30 s of landing; treat "30 s" as a design target, not a measured fact.
- Design thinking (decision + rationale + iteration) is the dominant criterion across all named practitioners.

### Gaps
- No credible measured data on time spent per portfolio; no GDC talk transcripts retrieved; Reddit threads could not be read in full.

## 2. Common mistakes and red flags

### Takeaway
Most-cited: no process/thinking (just screenshots or final result), too many or unrelated/weak projects, giant docs, art/polish over design, tutorial-derived work, broken links/poor site, generic non-tailored applications, vague accomplishments.

### Cited Findings
- "Glorified engine tutorials with polished art assets"; level prototypes emphasising aesthetics over functionality; relying on degrees/certificates instead of work; showing only final result — [80.lv 2025](https://80.lv/articles/why-game-design-applicants-aren-t-landing-interviews) (sponsored)
- Too many unrelated projects, work without context (wireframes/sticky notes unexplained), large text blocks, long skills/software lists, sharing via Dropbox instead of a designed portfolio, missing/poor/broken links — [Massive](https://www.massive.se/blog/recruitment/my-top-5-portfolio-tips-ux-game-design/)
- Generic CV to many companies; non-concise CV; copy-paste content; 40-page design docs; "drawings on napkins"; feedback only from friends/family; defensive reaction to criticism — [Game Developer, Creative Assembly 2018](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-portfolio-building-tips-from-a-creative-assembly-vet)
- Unfinished oversized projects, weak school assignments, massive design docs, heavy lore/worldbuilding, "ideas only" projects without playable work (WPI career center, May 2026) — [WPI CDC](https://cdc.wpi.edu/blog/2026/05/14/building-an-imgd-portfolio/)
- Weak portfolio website; relying on mission statement/extracurriculars; unfinished projects; unreliable team collaborators — [Levy 2013](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/breaking-into-game-design-part-2---build-your-portfolio)
- Haiku Games founder (May 2022): focusing on games you play instead of games you made; not knowing the company; vague accomplishments (quantify: player counts, ranking, shipped or not); not linking experience to role — [Haiku Games](https://www.haikugames.com/blog/the-top-4-mistakes-game-designer-job-applicants-make/)
- Filament Games HR (Sarah Conn, 2017): ignoring application requirements or omitting attachments ("Not a Fit" pile), not proofreading, mixing up company names, calling hiring managers, following up too soon (wait 1-2 weeks) — [Filament Games](https://www.filamentgames.com/blog/how-get-hired-game-studio-working-hr)
- Pinkus 2013: citing page counts, 30 docs but one shipped game, acting like a "#1 fan" rather than wanting to do great work — [Game Developer 2013](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/tips-for-game-designer-applications-interviews)
- Fred Z. (2024): resume hard to read/flashy, generic cover letter with no company name, overly long applications — [Substack](https://fredzed.substack.com/p/the-job-hunt-applications)

### Inferences
- Red flag list is consistent across 2013-2026; the core ones have not changed.

### Gaps
- No quantified ranking of which mistake is most common.

## 3. Communicating role in team projects

### Takeaway
State a specific "My role/My contribution" per project, name the systems/features you owned and credit teammates; use "I" not "we"; avoid vague "I worked on".

### Cited Findings
- WPI: every project should state your role and "exact contribution", plus tools used — [WPI CDC 2026](https://cdc.wpi.edu/blog/2026/05/14/building-an-imgd-portfolio/)
- Killick: use "I" rather than "we", since the reader is evaluating you — [PocketGamer.biz 2018](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/11-portfolio-tips-for-game-design-graduates/)
- Seemann: explain context, your role, challenges, collaboration — [Massive](https://www.massive.se/blog/recruitment/my-top-5-portfolio-tips-ux-game-design/)
- Game Design Skills (snippet): starter portfolios give summary responsibilities; advanced ones explain each contribution via result/goal/solution; vague statements signal a small contribution (snippet; sources low-authority) — [Game Design Skills](https://gamedesignskills.com/game-design/make-a-game-design-portfolio/)
- Levy 2013: team projects can fail when collaborators don't deliver; build small games alone unless you trust the team — [Game Developer 2013](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/breaking-into-game-design-part-2---build-your-portfolio)
- Haiku: quantify (team size, results) — [Haiku Games](https://www.haikugames.com/blog/the-top-4-mistakes-game-designer-job-applicants-make/)

### Inferences
- Combine a role line (role, team size, duration, tools, what you owned) at the top of each project with contribution-by-contribution case study.

### Gaps
- No named hiring manager quote specifically on attributing team work was found.

## 4. Differences by level and studio type

### Takeaway
Juniors: 2-4 polished projects, jam and student work accepted, focus on process and finishing. Seniors/mid: expected to show shipped work and ownership. Little sourced detail on AAA vs indie differences.

### Cited Findings
- Junior mix: 2-4 polished projects, one game jam game, one deeper case study; 3-5 strong projects overall; no need for 20 games, a commercial release or perfect website (WPI, 2026) — [WPI CDC](https://cdc.wpi.edu/blog/2026/05/14/building-an-imgd-portfolio/)
- Killick: released work > side projects > academic/jam; recency matters — [PocketGamer.biz 2018](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/11-portfolio-tips-for-game-design-graduates/)
- Most studios count student projects, jams and playable prototypes as junior experience; Unity/Unreal comfort usually enough (snippet) — [Generalist Programmer](https://generalistprogrammer.com/tutorials/game-developer-portfolio-and-resume-guide)
- Market context: junior hiring is tougher than ever (competitive industry, cyclical production, GenAI) (snippet) — [Techneeds 2025](https://www.techneeds.com/2025/12/19/5-steps-to-craft-an-impressive-video-game-portfolio/)
- Seniors: "next-level" portfolios explain each contribution by result/goal/solution (snippet) — [Game Design Skills](https://gamedesignskills.com/game-design/make-a-game-design-portfolio/)
- Tailor to studio: separate portfolios/sections for art vs design, mobile vs console — [PocketGamer.biz 2018](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/11-portfolio-tips-for-game-design-graduates/)

### Inferences
- Mid/senior detail (ownership, impact metrics, leadership) is inferred, not sourced.

### Gaps
- No source explicitly comparing AAA vs indie vs mobile expectations; no explicit senior vs junior hiring manager statement.

## 5. Resume/CV, cover letter, and link to the portfolio

### Takeaway
One concise page, relevant work first, links to portfolio prominently; portfolio proves what the resume claims; cover letter optional but must be specific; resume PDF on the site.

### Cited Findings
- CV one page, only role-relevant info (Mann) — [Game Developer 2018](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-portfolio-building-tips-from-a-creative-assembly-vet)
- Fred Z.: resume brief, readable over flashy, list what you've done with portfolio links; education/extracurriculars matter less; resume usually first thing the hiring team sees; cover letter mostly read by recruiters, ideally two paragraphs naming the company; "nothing sells you as well as what you've made"; referrals bypass recruiters — [Substack 2024](https://fredzed.substack.com/p/the-job-hunt-applications)
- Filament HR: "Relevant Experience" section, accomplishments not duties; portfolio "can often make or break your application" — [Filament 2017](https://www.filamentgames.com/blog/how-get-hired-game-studio-working-hr)
- Pinkus: don't lead with document counts; tie documentation to process and who used it; cover letter adds context rather than repeating resume — [Game Developer 2013](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/tips-for-game-designer-applications-interviews)
- WPI: downloadable PDF resume on an About/Resume page with contact info and LinkedIn; link LinkedIn to the rest — [WPI CDC](https://cdc.wpi.edu/blog/2026/05/14/building-an-imgd-portfolio/)
- Haiku: quantify accomplishments, link experience to role — [Haiku Games](https://www.haikugames.com/blog/the-top-4-mistakes-game-designer-job-applicants-make/)

### Inferences
- Resume = index into portfolio (project names with direct links); the portfolio must not just repeat the resume.

### Gaps
- No quote on ATS/keyword screening from a game-industry recruiter was verified.

## 6. Design tests and relation to the portfolio

### Takeaway
Tests are common for juniors, vary from ~4 h to ~10 days, and are criticised as long/unpaid; a strong portfolio should reduce the need; interviews probe your reasoning in the test.

### Cited Findings
- Fred Z. (Nov 2024, no formal credentials, says he took dozens and wrote some): most studios do tests badly; test should only be needed for entry/junior applicants; review the portfolio first and skip the test if sufficient; keep to ~4 h; he would end an application if a test exceeded ~8 h; ideas/first drafts are the easy part, handling revision is the job; follow-up interview with feedback/new constraints reveals more — [Substack](https://fredzed.substack.com/p/the-job-hunt-design-tests)
- Glassdoor anecdotes (snippet): Ubisoft Milan draft task with 3 game types plus questions explaining choices; Gameloft UI task for a chance-based mini-game (candidate thought it above junior level); Popcore ~4 h; Socialpoint ~10 days — [Glassdoor Ubisoft](https://static.glassdoor.com.br/Interview/Ubisoft-Interview-E12717-RVW37711969.htm), [Gameloft](https://www.glassdoor.sg/Interview/Gameloft-Interview-E18006-RVW23585114.htm), [Popcore](https://www.glassdoor.co.uk/Interview/Popcore-Interview-E3691207-RVW77220348.htm), [Socialpoint](https://www.glassdoor.com.au/Interview/Socialpoint-Interview-E662378-RVW40426672.htm)
- Live design scenarios in interviews on shipped games emphasise thought process (snippet) — [Game Design Skills](https://gamedesignskills.com/game-design/interview-questions/)

### Inferences
- Treat 1-2 portfolio case studies as a "pre-answered design test": same structure (goal, constraints, options, decision, iteration).

### Gaps
- No studio recruiter quoting their rubric was found; Glassdoor evidence is anecdotal.

## 7. Brazil / Latin America

### Takeaway
Few dedicated sources. Available advice: build portfolio and LinkedIn in English for international applications, show role/process/result, expect English interviews; local studios list portfolio (and sometimes English) as requirements.

### Cited Findings
- Universidade do Intercâmbio (updated 12 Oct 2026; commercial blog): "Monte o portfólio e o LinkedIn em inglês"; each project described with problem, role, process, result; train technical English (meetings, docs, feedback); 3-5 finished projects beat 20 incomplete; mentions Global Game Jam, itch.io, Abragames, Brazil Games, gamescom latam, Amir Satvat's data on job shifts (fetched) — [Universidade do Intercâmbio](https://www.universidadedointercambio.com/trabalhar-com-games-exterior-portfolio/)
- Search summary (snippet): bilingual resume recommended; many remote roles use async tests/case studies; interview-level English a basic requirement — [DIO](https://www.dio.me/en/articles/carreira-internacional-em-tecnologia-como-se-destacar-e-conquistar-uma-vaga-fora-do-brasil-46f4c8b0520b), [Cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/pt-BR/blog/2026-07-02-game-industry-layoffs-getting-rehired)
- Wildlife Studios (São Paulo) listings asked for strong oral/written English and an online portfolio; Pipa Studios senior role expected a portfolio presentation; Aquiris (expired 2022) wanted a portfolio showing planning and execution of levels (snippets from job aggregators) — [Wildlife/Greenhouse](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/wildlifestudios/jobs/8448666002), [Pipa/Outscal](https://outscal.com/job/senior-game-designer-at-pipa-studios-in-so-paulo-so-paulo-state-of-so-paulo-brazil), [Aquiris/Hitmarker](https://hitmarker.net/jobs/aquiris-level-designer-904225)
- Market context: Amir Satvat's data cited by GamesBeat: widening the search beyond games improves odds of finding any job (~5x per one summary) — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/your-odds-of-finding-a-job-in-games-amir-satvats-project-flagpole)

### Inferences
- English as primary portfolio language with optional PT-BR toggle is consistent with sources; for Brazilian-only studios, PT copy is probably acceptable but no source states this.

### Gaps
- No hiring-manager source from a Brazilian/LatAm studio on PT vs EN preferences; no data on visa/relocation or timezone preferences for remote roles. Commercial sources, low authority.
